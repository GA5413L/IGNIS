**Subject:** Would you consider testing a small research tool for microgravity flame-spread data? (about an hour, later this year)

Dear [title and name],

I am [your name and role] and I am taking part in the 2026 NASA Space Apps Challenge, a hackathon. The project is not affiliated with or endorsed by NASA. I am writing because of your work on [their field], which I found at [where you found their contact details].

We plan to build a small research aid for published microgravity combustion data. It starts with NASA's BASS-II experiments on PMMA rods aboard the ISS (Link et al., *Scientific Reports* 2018; NASA/TM-20210011385). The idea is narrow. When someone combines records from different tests, the tool keeps each record's experimental history attached:
- which physical specimen it came from;
- whether that specimen had been burned before;
- what atmosphere the test started in;
- where the sources disagree.

That way a minimum or a comparison is not silently built on a record that should be qualified.

We don't know yet whether this helps more than a well-organised spreadsheet holding the same records, and we want to test that fairly. Once a working prototype exists [after the hackathon on 14–15 November 2026 / a date you can commit to], would you consider spending about 60–90 minutes on a few research questions about these data, half with the prototype and half with the spreadsheet? We would record only your answers, the time taken and your comments. You could stop at any point, and we would not name you without your permission.

This is only a question about whether you might be available. There is nothing to do now. A short reply ("perhaps later", "no", or the name of someone better placed) would be very helpful.

Thank you for considering it.
