## (a) Anchors

### DSC-01. SAME authors conclude that detectors measuring more than one moment of the smoke size distribution may detect better and reject false alarms better than single-moment detectors
- **Primary quote (author interpretation):** "These results suggest that detection methods that can measure more than one moment of the size distribution may show more successful detection and false alarm rejection than single moment detectors." [SAME-2 paper, PDF p. 12]
- **Supporting (observed result, then interpretation):** "For the conditions of the SAME experiment, all samples produced significant numbers of sub-micron particulate that are readily detected using an ionization smoke detector. However, the aging results suggest that some materials such as silicone rubber can produce smoke that can reach sizes better detected by light scattering techniques." [SAME-2 paper, PDF p. 12]
- **Supporting (author interpretation of the prototype):** "The results from the prototype 2-moment detector (MPASS) were very encouraging in this respect with the technique showing real potential as a means to respond to a broad particle range while avoiding excessive false alarms from nuisance sources." [SAME-2 paper, PDF p. 12]
- **Source:** Particle Morphology and Size Results from the Smoke Aerosol Measurement Experiment-2 (AIAA 2012-3536, 42nd ICES, 2012). NTRS 20130000422. https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20130000422/downloads/20130000422.pdf (access: full text, public NTRS PDF (13 pp.))
- **Scope and cautions:** Pyrolysis (pre-ignition, non-flaming) smoke from small heated samples in the Microgravity Science Glovebox; five materials; hedged wording ("suggest", "may"). It is not a statement that ionization or light-scattering detectors fail, and it is not a qualified detector design. MPASS was a technology demonstration.
- **Panel:** detection

### DSC-02. In one Saffire VI test, a far-field smoke alarm threshold was reached at 981 s, after most of the hazardous gas release
- **Primary quote (observed result):** "The smoke from the fire event was readily detected by an ionization detector and a light scattering system but a typical alarm threshold was only achieved at 981 seconds. By this time most of the hazardous gas release had occurred." [Saffire VI preliminary, PDF p. 6]
- **Supporting (method, the threshold was chosen by the authors):** "Selecting 1 mg/m3 as a relatively tight alarm threshold, the FFD detector reached that threshold at 981 seconds." [Saffire VI preliminary, PDF p. 5]
- **Source:** Preliminary Results from the Saffire VI Experiment (ICES-2024-365). NTRS 20240002981. https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20240002981/downloads/Saffire-ICES-2024-ICES-365-accepted%20final.pdf (access: full text, public NTRS PDF (7 pp.))
- **Scope and cautions:** Figure 7 of the paper identifies the test as Saffire VI-3 (two-sided PMMA, concurrent flow) in an uncrewed Cygnus; the detector was in the Far Field Diagnostics unit at the other end of the vehicle; atmosphere conditions are in the paper's Table 1 (PDF p. 3, not transcribed here). One test, one vehicle packing, one sensor location, a 1 mg/m3 threshold selected for the analysis. Not a general detection time and not a statement about ISS or Orion detectors. "Preliminary" is in the paper title.

## (b) State of the science (for the lab head)

<!--SOS-->
**Detection.** SAME flight measurements (2007, 2010) of pre-ignition smoke from overheated spacecraft materials show that particle size depends on material, ageing and airflow: Kapton and Teflon smokes were small, lamp-wick and silicone smokes large, and no-flow heating gave larger particles. The authors conclude that single-moment detectors (Shuttle ionization, ISS forward light scattering) each respond best to part of that range, and that sensing more than one moment may improve detection and false-alarm rejection. DAFT found ISS background particulate very low. In Saffire IV to VI fires (1.5 to 3.2 kW), carbon monoxide and carbon dioxide, not heat or pressure, were the significant habitability impact, and a far-field particulate threshold was reached only after much of the gas release. The authors ask for lower thresholds or more detectors.

**Aftermath.** The Saffire IV and V smoke eater did not remove CO as expected, and released acid gas was not seen at the far-field sensors. Both remain unresolved.

**Suppression.** The FLEX report archives methanol and n-heptane droplet data under nitrogen and carbon dioxide dilution without interpreting them; helium appears in later FLEX material. Its signature result is cool-flame burning after hot-flame radiative extinction, flagged as a re-ignition concern that existing kinetic models do not predict accurately. Mist reported water-mist suppression of premixed propane-air flames. An engineering unit of the ISS fine-water-mist extinguisher extinguished nearly all test-standard fires.

**Not established by these sources:** partial-gravity behaviour, any agent ranking for cabin fires, and transfer of droplet or gas-flame extinction limits to habitat design.
<!--/SOS-->

### Chemistry note

<!--CHEM-->
Low-temperature chemistry enters where the hot flame ends: FLEX droplets kept burning after radiative extinction in a regime "characterized by negative-temperature-coefficient (NTC) chemistry" [Cool-flame extinction paper, PDF p. 1]. The investigators' simplified model is a six-step scheme (hydrogen abstraction, oxygen addition, RO2-to-QOOH isomerization, second oxygen addition, ketohydroperoxide decomposition; FLEX presentation, PDF p. 9), and they state that detailed mechanisms mispredict second-stage extinction (gap G2). Context without an anchor: rate constants for such isomerization and decomposition steps are commonly obtained from quantum-chemistry calculations, but no full-text source retrieved here shows that link for FLEX or CFI models, so the app should not claim it.
<!--/CHEM-->

Sources for the note: Cool-flame extinction during n-alkane droplet combustion in microgravity (Central States Section of the Combustion Institute, March 2014). NTRS 20140006750. https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20140006750/downloads/20140006750.pdf (access: full text, public NTRS PDF (9 pp.), retrieved this session; abstract and concluding remarks read); Isolated Single Droplet Combustion: FLEX Experiments onboard the International Space Station (JASMAC-33 presentation, 15 Oct 2021). NTRS 20210022617. https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210022617/downloads/JASMAC-33%20FLEX%20w%20video.pdf (access: presentation slides, public NTRS PDF (17 pp.); slide text only) The scheme heading on the slide reads "6-step simplified chemical kinetics (based on San Diego Mechanism)" [FLEX presentation, PDF p. 9].
