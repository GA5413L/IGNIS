## 3. Comparison, inverse inference and uncertainty

A useful observation model is the **author-defined schematic** `observed response = measurement operator(physical-model output) + model discrepancy + observation error`. Its purpose is bookkeeping; it is not an IGNIS fire equation. Record what was measured and processed before calculating a residual.

Calibration is an inverse problem. A good fit may identify a useful predictive combination of parameters without identifying each physical parameter. For example, in the purely synthetic relation `y = a × b`, `(a,b)=(1,2)` and `(2,1)` produce the same observation. That simple ambiguity is why reconstructing an unknown source from remote sensors needs information about transport, yields and losses, not a fluent answer. Brynjarsdóttir and O'Hagan show how unmodelled discrepancy can bias calibration and why adding a weakly specified discrepancy term does not automatically justify physical-parameter inference or extrapolation. Their author manuscript was read at pp.1–5 and 22–24; no calibration model was fitted here. [Author manuscript](https://www.tonyohagan.co.uk/academic/pdf/simmach.pdf)

Retain the useful distinctions in T1–T4:

- An imposed initial offset makes the initial residual zero by construction; later change/shape can still be examined conditionally.
- A source total scaled using a target-derived total cannot independently validate that total. Other output comparisons can remain informative with their shared dependencies recorded.
- A published, initial-mass-referenced balance signal can be compared under its recorded convention; it cannot silently become isolated remaining PMMA mass.
- Sensible heat added to a cabin and chemical heat release can share units without sharing the measurement operator or boundary.

Use a small **information-use ledger**: publication → campaign → specimen/burn/segment/channel/window; source and model versions; shared calibration/apparatus; each datum's roles as boundary/source input, initialization, fit, selection, post-processing or evaluation; and whether that use is known or unknown. Multiple roles are permitted. Hiding a plotted sensor or moving a publication to another split does not erase its prior use.

JCGM 100 §§5.2 and F.1.2 identify common instruments, standards and reference data as sources of correlated uncertainty. The existing identity `Var(B−A)=Var(B)+Var(A)−2Cov(A,B)` is appropriate when those random quantities and second moments are defined. The IGNIS rule remains: an unreported covariance is not evidence of independence. A sensitivity calculation with explicit covariance assumptions can be useful; a fabricated distribution or confidence band cannot. Source spread, measurement uncertainty, model discrepancy, numerical tolerance and uncertainty about an interpretation are separate fields. [JCGM 100:2008](https://www.bipm.org/documents/20126/2071204/JCGM1002008E.pdf/cb0ef43f-baa5-11cf-3f85-4dcd86f77bd6)

## 4. The mathematical contribution, corrected

### 4.1 Three different frontiers/claims

| Object | What is optimized or ordered | Claim that could be earned |
|---|---|---|
| **Condition-gap skyline** | Absolute documented gaps from one stated target, within eligible and equally documented strata | Which finite records are nondominated under the declared gap policy. No comparative scientific usefulness or predictive accuracy follows automatically. |
| **System-performance Pareto set** | Useful correct task completion, complete-task latency, cost and researcher correction effort, after eligibility gates | Observed nondominated configurations for the evaluated tasks, versions, budgets and objectives. Statistical dominance may remain unresolved. |
| **Predictive-physics advance** | Error/uncertainty on a specified physical quantity against reproduced physical/statistical baselines and independent relevant evidence | Improved prediction for the evaluated domain. This requires numerical model/data work that has not been performed here. |

The historical two-runtime results remain frozen. Both configurations failed their quality gate, so their admissible system frontier is empty, even if their raw vectors trade objectives. The new condition-gap module cannot change that result. Skyline operations are established prior art; the canonical 2001 paper's bibliography was verified, not re-read in full. [Authors' institution bibliography](https://publications.systems.ethz.ch/author/109/Stephan%20B%C3%B6rzs%C3%B6nyi)

### 4.2 Eligibility and meaning precede dominance

For each requested use, apply the master specification's three-valued prerequisites first. A known contradiction blocks that use; a necessary unknown leaves it unresolved. Keep the source finding and useful narrower routes. Numerical-transfer eligibility additionally requires the method's acquired and reproduced implementation. Missing pressure need not prevent an ordinary summary.

Within a use cohort, group by endpoint/operator/configuration/regime when these affect the meaning of comparison, then by the set of documented target axes. Across different missingness signatures, report **incomparable under this policy**. Do not fill missing values with zero or infinity. Do not interpret a singleton frontier as unusually strong evidence. Counterevidence and explicitly requested studies remain in the assessment even when condition-dominated.

The existing signature grouping avoids a real mathematical trap. Comparing only shared coordinates can cycle: with smaller preferred, `A=(1,?,3)`, `B=(2,1,?)`, `C=(?,2,2)` gives A better than B, B better than C, and C better than A on their respective shared coordinates. Such a relation cannot justify the module's transitivity-based algorithm. Equal-signature grouping is a defensible conservative policy, not a complete solution to missing data.

Closeness also needs a purpose. Absolute gaps erase which side of the target a condition occupies; keep the signed differences. A U-shaped flammability boundary, a regime change or a material mismatch prevents interpreting “closer in oxygen/pressure/flow” as “more useful” without an additional contract. Axis selection itself is a product/scientific policy even when there are no numerical weights.

### 4.3 What the exact finite-set algorithm proves

For a fixed nonempty stratum with finite nonnegative gaps, define `a ≺ b` iff every gap of a is no larger and at least one is smaller. Identical vectors tie. Lexicographic sorting places every dominator before its dominated point. A later point cannot dominate an earlier accepted frontier point; if an earlier dominator was itself rejected, transitivity gives a retained frontier dominator. Hence sorting followed by comparison with all retained frontier members produces the exact finite skyline and a valid witness for every rejected point.

This argument applies to the declared numeric gap vectors, not to physical validity. The implementation's “one scan” still searches the retained frontier for each candidate: worst-case work is `O(d n²)` plus sorting, not linear time. All n points can be nondominated. At the present small corpus size, an independent pairwise oracle is simpler to audit than a more elaborate algorithm.

**Scalarization, forward direction.** If a score strictly increases whenever any one gap increases while the others stay fixed, every exact minimum is nondominated: a dominator would have a strictly smaller score. For a merely nondecreasing score, dominated points can tie; a nonempty finite set has at least one nondominated minimizer. Randomly testing four score families is evidence about those examples, not a proof about every increasing function.

**Scalarization, converse.** For a selected nondominated vector f in a finite set, choose positive same-axis offsets `s_i` and set `r_i(x)=(x_i+s_i)/(f_i+s_i)`. Identical vectors remain tied. Every distinct candidate x has `max_i r_i(x)>1`. Let `delta` be the minimum positive excess over the finite candidate set, and d the number of axes. Then

```text
S_f(x) = max_i r_i(x) + epsilon × sum_i r_i(x)
0 < epsilon < delta/d
```

is strictly increasing and selects f over every distinct vector: its maximum-term advantage exceeds any possible reduction of the sum term, since all ratios are positive. If there are no distinct candidates, any positive epsilon works. This is a finite-set existence argument with a dataset-dependent bound. It does not identify the score a researcher should prefer.

**The current fixed epsilon is not universal.** The existing test uses `s_i=1` and `epsilon=1e−9`. Synthetic gaps `f=(1,1)` and `b=(1.0000000001,0)` are both nondominated, but its score gives:

```text
S_f(f) = 1.000000002
S_f(b) = 1.00000000155...
S_f(b) − S_f(f) = −8999999999 / 20000000000000000000
```

The counterexample survives the module's 12-significant-digit cleaning when produced from target `(34,56.5)` and synthetic points `(35,57.5)` / `(35.0000000001,56.5)`. No experimental record is implied. The test's “theorem” title and fixed-epsilon construction need correction; the underlying skyline algorithm does not fail because of this counterexample.

Positive weighted sums also need not expose every frontier point. In `{(0,3),(2,2),(3,0)}`, the middle vector is nondominated, but making it beat both others would require simultaneously `w1≤w2/2` and `w2≤w1/2`, impossible for positive weights. Such a point is unsupported **by linear scalarization**; that term says nothing about evidential support.

### 4.4 Numeric and uncertainty contract before integration

1. Validate finite target values, canonical units/bases, unique record IDs, allowed axes and finite computed differences. The coordinator reproduced NaN/Infinity targets producing invalid gaps/frontier membership. The source has no finite-target guard. Even finite operands can overflow on subtraction. Invalid supplied data must be distinguishable from an unreported measurement.
2. Separate display rounding from comparison policy. `toPrecision(12)` can erase a small real ordering difference; it is not a measurement-uncertainty model. Exact printed-decimal arithmetic or a documented precision/interval policy is preferable. Require invariance to record permutation and ordinary unit changes, tie retention and valid dominance witnesses.
3. Positive affine unit conversion gives `gap' = a × gap` and preserves exact dominance when target and data are converted together. Applying a strictly increasing transformation to each **gap** also preserves dominance. A nonlinear transformation of the original values followed by recomputed absolute gaps need not preserve proximity across opposite sides of the target. Kelvin/Celsius or Pa/kPa display changes must not change the scientific decision.
4. Correlated axes do not invalidate Pareto order mathematically. An exact duplicate axis adds no new ordering information; treating derived oxygen partial pressure alongside oxygen fraction and total pressure as independent evidence is unjustified. Composite derived gaps can change the frontier. Avoid padding the axis list with all available variables or interpreting a large frontier as corroboration.
5. For documented gap enclosures `[L_ai,U_ai]`, `U_ai≤L_bi` on every axis with strict inequality on one is a sufficient **robust dominance** condition. It is conservative when shared-target/covariance constraints restrict the joint possibilities. Overlap usually leaves dominance unresolved. These enclosures are not confidence intervals unless their probabilistic construction is justified. Unknown uncertainty gets an explicit status; it does not become a zero-width interval.

The standalone exact-rational harness passed on **766 finite synthetic subsets** (511 two-dimensional subsets and 255 three-dimensional subsets), including 1,058 adaptive-scalarizer checks, input reversal, positive axis scaling and dominance-oracle agreement. A separate JavaScript arithmetic check reproduced the fixed-epsilon failure. These are bounded development checks plus the arguments above. They are not exhaustive application tests, an independently reviewed theorem, or experimental validation.

## 5. A finite evaluation that can earn a useful claim

**Protocol proposal `completion-math-v1`: NOT FROZEN / NOT RUN.** This does not replace any historical evaluation or relax the failed runtime gate.

Use 24 task episodes, four each for ordinary lookup, direct conditional comparison, positive published-transfer planning, invalid stronger transfer with a useful alternative, genuine missing prerequisites, and revision. Include prevention/detection/suppression when claiming that breadth. Group assignment and uncertainty analysis by underlying experiment/model/calibration lineage. Paraphrases, repeated requests, sensors and related publications do not create independent samples. Reserve genuinely different investigations for later generalization claims; if those assets are unavailable, report a development study.

Run four primary arms on identical reviewed facts, source access, qualifications and rule descriptions: (A) a usable worksheet, (B) a strong ordinary retrieval/synthesis assistant, (C) a typed-form deterministic rules interface, and (D) complete IGNIS. That is **96 assigned task episodes**, including failed/aborted cases. Match the language model and permitted calls/token/retrieval budget wherever comparing B and D. Treat time spent curating rules as a separate measured input, then perform a separate original-document comparison if claiming end-to-end superiority. A PaperQA2/OpenScholar-class reproduction is an additional prerequisite for a broad scientific-RAG SOTA claim; this pilot is not a substitute for running one.

For mechanism diagnosis, freeze a balanced 12-episode subset before running four one-component removals: domain/endpoint enforcement, lineage/information-use checks, frontier display, and automatic revision. This is **48 further episodes**, scored against the full-system results on the same subset. Preserve all other information and prompts. A loss after removing domain checks supports those checks; it does not establish a new skyline algorithm. If the frontier does not improve correct selection or effort, omit it from the main workflow.

**Proposed engineering gates:** no fabricated source/data or unsupported numerical transfer; no wrong endpoint/operator binding or independent-validation overclaim; all designated positive-transfer cases return the supported narrower route; all designated missingness cases preserve unknowns; all revision cases identify the changed premise and affected conclusion; at least 23/24 episodes fully useful and correct under the prespecified rubric. These thresholds are prospective product gates, not population-reliability guarantees. An unresolved answer can pass when it identifies the necessary missing information and retains the available useful result; blanket refusal cannot pass answerable cases.

After these gates, compare useful-correct episode count, total researcher correction time, complete-task latency (including failed tasks and repairs), and full cost per assigned task. Report critical-error counts and inappropriate exclusions separately. Do not optimize the number of generated claims. An observed nondominated configuration is relative to this finite set. Inferential dominance requires an analysis of paired task outcomes, meaningful margins and uncertainty; a tiny difference of sample means is insufficient. With few independent lineages, raw paired outcomes may be more defensible than a bootstrap confidence claim.

The evaluation design is supported by two complementary scientific-AI precedents. PaperQA2 separates accuracy over all questions from precision on answered questions and checks source DOI attribution; its methods include newly authored post-development questions. OpenScholar evaluates citation support/coverage and content quality separately and acknowledges that preference/usefulness ratings can emphasize writing over factual verification. Neither benchmark directly tests IGNIS's measurement operators, calibration reuse or physical transfer domains. Add those rubric items explicitly and require actual qualified adjudicators for an external-validity claim. Multiple AI reviewers remain AI self-checks. [PaperQA2 v2, §§2,8.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13740v2), [OpenScholar v1, §4 and Appendix B.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.14199v1)

Kapoor and Narayanan's leakage taxonomy further supports keeping preprocessing, tuning and selection outside the test information, checking outcome-proxy features, and matching test dependence to the claimed population. Do not apply a random row split when claiming performance on new campaigns or materials. [Published paper, taxonomy L1–L3; PDF pp.5–6](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10513990)

Before executing a transfer model: review all equations visually, pin constants/code/data/solver versions, reproduce a published reference result within justified source rounding/precision, and test its documented failure region. Reference reproduction is still not external validation. Before claiming advantage: complete the matched prospective study and real independent adjudication. Before claiming adoption: observe a researcher successfully using/exporting a task and measure correction/maintenance cost.

If the worksheet matches IGNIS, the curated contracts can remain the valuable contribution. If a domain-aware planner measurably reduces unsupported transfer while preserving useful completion, that is a credible narrow scientific software result. Neither outcome promises a competition award or universal SOTA.
