## SAME: stronger ancestry, plus a feasible operation now

The newly acquired final report explicitly states **67 SAME-R runs, including two using reused samples**, with secondary-aging observations listed separately to produce **109 entries**; it reports 70 collected TEM samples. Nominal carousel numbers 7(4) through 11(4) represent successive loadings of physical carousel 4. This supplies real dependence/ancestry information. It does not establish a row-by-row crosswalk to the combined workbook, whose known CSV/workbook identity mismatch remains unresolved. [SAME-R final report, PSI-101 file above, PDF p. 3](https://psi.nasa.gov/physci/repo/data/investigations/PSI-101)

The report also distinguishes moment instruments from flight smoke detectors. It reports first-moment ionization calibration-range exceedance for many smoke aerosols, whereas its diameter-of-average-mass calculation does not use that first moment. Dilution settings and material-specific third-moment calibration therefore matter when interpreting size summaries. It does **not** establish an ISS obscuration-channel calibration change between the two flights in the inspected main text. The flight-dependent voltage pattern found by the mathematical specialist must remain a configuration/offset/window question. [Same report, PDF pp. 24–26](https://psi.nasa.gov/physci/repo/data/investigations/PSI-101)

**Implementable contract: `same-paired-channel-audit-v1`.** Select the pinned combined workbook, flight, material, DBP inclusion policy, two channel IDs and two independently declared finite nonnegative voltage cutoffs. Pair exclusively by workbook hash + worksheet + Excel row. Return the four joint categories, each channel's own finite denominator, the jointly finite denominator, one-/both-unavailable counts split by missing/error/non-numeric status, exact source-cell witnesses, recorded instrument status and flight/material strata. Equality belongs to at-or-above; zero and negative finite source values remain recorded values. Explicit exclusions require row IDs and reasons. Export all selected rows, including unavailable pairs.

The coordinated [source-only recount](../../../_raw/professional_research_2026-11-15/ml/available_data_audit.json) gives an immediate useful answer for AW scatter / AV obscuration, separately selected 2 V cutoffs, DBP excluded: **134 rows, 133 jointly finite; 82 of those 133 rows lie on opposite sides of the two cutoffs**. All finite flight-2 AV readings are below this cutoff and all finite flight-1 AV readings are at-or-above it. A pooled percentage would conceal that strong flight structure. This is a prompt to inspect source configuration and offsets, not evidence of alarm disagreement, detector failure or a common physical threshold. The mathematical specialist supplies the four cells, bounds and witnesses; this report supplies the newly recovered measurement/ancestry context.

Required before stronger claims: channel transfer functions, enabled/status definitions, source and integration windows, offsets, device/calibration identities, run/sample/aging lineage and the intended physical quantity. Falsifiers include changed source bytes, a corrected measurement definition, or a reconciled ancestry that changes the selected observation set. Do not fit an alarm classifier to these descriptive cutoffs.

## TEM aging files: useful data, unresolved processing choices

The selected pair contains **14 unaged and 15 aged worksheets**, not two clean tables of independent experiments. `Comments on binned data!A5` identifies TEM sizing; `A2` gives a 0.010 µm minimum particle size and a log-bin width. `all data!C5/C6` labels/stores an average diameter, while separate “big only” and binned sheets express different retained populations. Nine same-named sheets have identical stored cell records across the two files, including template/result sheets and notes. Both contain cached `#DIV/0!` cells. These are preserved source facts, not repaired automatically.

One consequential example is aged `Binned data (2)!B5=870` versus `D5=869`, under `# of particles` and `dN`; the unaged counterpart stores 1803 in both cells. The source's “issue with bins” filename is consistent with a need for review, but does not identify the cause. We do not choose one total, recompute a distribution, or turn particle counts into experiment replicates.

The final report links Kapton run 62 to TEM aging images and reports microscope/camera calibration using a MagiCal reference; this is promising ancestry evidence, not a complete particle-to-detector join. A publishable comparison needs the TEM grid/image IDs, retained particle set, scale calibration, segmentation/exclusion rule, bin edges, source-generation event, collection windows and an explicit diameter operator. The immediate product is a **processing-choice audit**, showing original counts/headers/errors and the difference between microscopy-derived particle sizes and moment-instrument quantities. [SAME-R final report, PDF pp. 7–8 and 16](https://psi.nasa.gov/physci/repo/data/investigations/PSI-101)

## NIST: a separate alarm measurement chain with usable positive answers

This is a better calibration/validation example than importing an arbitrary voltage threshold. NIST TN 1947 describes cotton-smolder smoke, smoke-box flow, orientation, repeat testing and optical/MIC checks. Its actual alarm endpoint is observed audibility, recorded manually and through a sound switch; it is distinct from a voltage cutoff. The reported uncertainties include 1 s alarm time, 4% relative obscuration and 0.2 pA MIC current. [Data dictionary](https://www.nist.gov/el/fire/engineered-fire-safety/data-smoke-alarm-performance), [TN 1947, PDF pp. 18–19 and 35 / printed pp. 8–9 and 25](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/TechnicalNotes/NIST.TN.1947.pdf)

For a reproducible source-only example, worksheet **`I 01-14`, row 8, A8=`I-01-6`** contains:

| Source configuration | Cells | Alarm time, s | Obscuration, %/ft | MIC, pA |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Worst orientation, repeat 1 | B8:D8 | 203 | 1.08 | 62.2 |
| Worst orientation, repeat 2 | E8:G8 | 199 | 1.01 | 59.7 |
| Worst orientation, repeat 3 | H8:J8 | 185 | 1.11 | 62.3 |
| Worst orientation, twice the flow | K8:M8 | 204 | 1.22 | 64.3 |
| Best orientation | N8:P8 | 135 | 0.69 | 67.7 |

These are the workbook's stored observations, not a causal effect estimate, modern product recommendation or independent-device sample of size five. A useful new operation is **inspect repeated configuration measurements for one identified unit**, showing source values and uncertainty conventions before any aggregation. The room sheets additionally identify fuel/nuisance context and experiment filenames: `Room I01-14!G4=FFoamG#T#`, with G5:I5 giving `1,1`, `1,2`, `1,3` for E5=`I01-6`. That is a documented path to individual room histories if later retrieval is authorized and useful.

Preserve model, physical unit, repeat, orientation, flow, smoke source, alarm endpoint, clock origin and apparatus. NIST obscuration in %/ft and MIC current in pA must never be pooled with SAME AV/BK volts. Ground results do not supply a microgravity transfer function. No smoke-box/room archive was needed for this example; raw time-series availability is **documented, not inspected here**.

## Saffire: actual telemetry exists, but pairing by row is wrong

The acquired Saffire-I workbook supplies detector voltage streams and explicitly unit-labeled pressure/temperature fields. `RawData` contains 9,750 populated rows including the header; `SaffireData` contains 6,084. These are telemetry records, not burns. The source README identifies SIBAL material, sample/camera structure and pre-/post-flow records. It concerns Saffire-I/III, not the later IV–VI gas snapshots already used by IGNIS.

**A concrete clock audit result:** `CygnusSmokeDetectorData` contains side-by-side arrays with separate clocks. At row 3, obscuration has **A3=1465931204893, C3=9283.18 s, D3=−0.005 V**; scatter has **F3=1465932848063, H3=10926.35 s, I3=0.02 V**. Pairing D3 and I3 by row would join different times. These cells are not a detector-delay measurement. Timestamp equality, sampling windows and an explicit alignment policy are prerequisites.

The same workbook exposes a further unit/status question: `RawData!A1` says `RelativeSeconds`, while derived sheets explicitly distinguish milliseconds and seconds. Initial radiometer/anemometer values can be large and negative. Preserve headers and stored values until clock, zero/scale, sentinel/status and calibration definitions are resolved. A radiometer channel is not chemical HRR, and no CO/CO2/HCN history was established from the inspected headers. The next feasible operation is a **clock and channel-availability inspector**, with exact timestamp witnesses and unpaired records, before any latency, heat-release or exposure calculation. [PSI-98 telemetry and README, exact filenames in the acquisition table](https://psi.nasa.gov/physci/repo/data/investigations/PSI-98)

## FLEX endpoint opportunity and limits of this search

The existing FLEX CSV supplies 274 rows, initial conditions, visible-flame extinction diameter and source end labels. It does not supply an independently adjudicated cool-flame cessation label. The primary detailed report sometimes explicitly leaves cool-flame status unresolved: FLEX-120's description says the available data were insufficient and its LLUV imager recorded no burn images. An endpoint ledger could distinguish visible-flame loss, observed subsequent reaction, and unassessable cool-flame status, preserving the test identifier and source-page evidence. No automatic CSV/report crosswalk or new cool-flame labels were created here. [NASA/TP-2015-216046, Figure 124, printed p. 148 / PDF p. 154](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20150023456/downloads/20150023456.pdf)

This bounded search did not obtain calibrated IV–VI gas/HRR histories, a channel-specific SAME alarm mapping, or a clean TEM analysis crosswalk. Those are unfilled data requirements, not claims of data absence. The new scripts are `fetch_selected.py`, `inspect_sources.py` and `build_opportunity_catalog.py`; they and the eight originals, per-response logs, cached-cell inventories and PDF/DOCX text extracts remain under `_raw/professional_research_2026-11-15/data/`. The catalog builder verified all eight original byte counts and hashes. These artifacts are reviewable inputs to future curation; no new application feature is claimed as implemented by this report.
