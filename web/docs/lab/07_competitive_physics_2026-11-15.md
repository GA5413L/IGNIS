# Competitive scientific audit against the implemented IGNIS

15 November 2026. Specialist AI research-team review, not independent human acceptance. Public-source snapshot; no competitor code, tests, saved models, or paid inference executed. No deployment or external account changes. The repositories below are relevant public projects, not independently verified competition entrants.

**Finding:** the strongest inspected alternatives are substantially more than paper-only prototypes. MARGIN already has seven versioned Saffire cases and sensitivity analysis; HALO has an integrated evidence brief and a fitted droplet model; MicroFire-V2 has careful admission and claim-support rules; IGNIS LAB has raw-history refits and grouped predictive evaluations; Kenyalang Blue Flame has a larger partial-gravity literature layer and an explicit test planner. Current IGNIS has a useful, narrower contribution: material/side-scoped condition comparisons, cell-preserving detector analysis, and revisioned checks on the quantity and observation operator a proposed model comparison would require. This review does not establish a best project, predictive SOTA, scientific certification, or likely judging outcome.

## 3. Consequential scientific findings

### MARGIN: a strong evidence-comparison implementation, with broad eligibility

**Source-inspected strengths.** Gravity must match categorically; planned evidence and limit values are separated; a missing O2 value cannot become a placeable case. The distance uses O2 differences, a logarithmic pressure ratio and flow differences, with each contribution available for inspection. Missing axes are omitted and flagged; current verdict output includes a nearest complete case. The UI explicitly says its DIRECT/NEAR/DISTANT classes are method-defined, and its robustness panel discloses when every combination remains a partial comparison. Criticism of an older silent missing-axis result should not be carried forward as if these changes did not exist. [Gate][M-gate], [distance][M-distance], [verdict][M-verdict], [inspector, lines 144–219][M-inspector].

The current curation uses 2026 Saffire values and marks flow direction unknown because sources disagree. It also cites 2021 Table 1 for IV-1, IV-2, V-2 and V-3, supplementing the five-case overlap in the 2024 source. This is useful additional context for IGNIS to review against the primary 2021 table before adding it. MARGIN correctly treats some 5 mm PMMA samples as thermally uncertain rather than assuming thickness alone settles thermal behavior. [Curated source][M-curated].

**Remaining limits, not a dismissal of ranking.** A broad solid-fuel/thermal class is not an exact material, burning-side, geometry or observation-endpoint cohort. The suppression gate admits liquid droplets and solid classes without checking for a suppression intervention or comparable suppression endpoint. Thus an admitted record can be relevant background without testing suppressant effectiveness. Also, the pipeline's BASS-II and Saffire-I nominal pressures are labeled from paper paraphrases whose quotes remain unverified. These conditions deserve visible qualification before physical transfer; none is a reason to declare all judgment-based relevance invalid. [Gate, lines 4–43][M-gate], [pipeline, lines 103–124][M-pipeline].

**IGNIS implication:** preserve the narrower material/side cohort and model-use checks. Add a clear account of why candidates were excluded, taking MARGIN's decomposed explanation and sensitivity UX seriously. Do not advertise the absence of scalar weighting as scientific accuracy by itself.

### HALO: useful integrated brief; alarm and extinction meanings need tighter boundaries

**Source-inspected strengths.** Inverse burner oxygen is excluded from ambient coverage, detector rows are kept out of the flame-coverage set, measured versus assumed pressure affects grading, and no SAME analog gives no detector result. Its detector-combination denominator requires every selected channel to be present; it does not silently count missing values as misses. [Ingestion][H-ingest], [method][H-method], [detector engine][H-detection].

**Detector interpretation issue.** The 2 V scatter cutoff is explicitly a working proxy; the ionization cutoff is the Lampwick lower tenth percentile. Nevertheless, the method text proceeds from below-proxy voltage to a statement that the detector would not alarm, and the brief asks whether a selected detector would notice. A proxy-count operation does not establish calibrated alarm sensitivity. The source extractor also drops workbook cell/flight/specimen/aging identities from its compact per-material output. IGNIS retains those identifiers and describes the 2 V operation as a selected cutoff, which is a concrete advantage for auditing. [Extractor][H-detector-extract], [method, lines 31–43][H-method], [card][H-detector-card].

**Recount:** HALO's saved values contain Kapton **20/35 below** 2 V and **15/35 at or above**, out of 36 total rows; Teflon is **18/23 below**, **5/23 at or above**. These match IGNIS's descriptive below-cutoff counts. Do not call 20/35 a detected fraction. HALO's engine computes the appropriate side of the threshold; this is not evidence of an engine arithmetic failure. Our recount also preserves the distinction between workbook rows and independent trials. [Saved detector data][H-detector-data]; local `descriptive_audit.json`.

**Extinction endpoint issue.** Its code fits a near-unregularized logistic model to N2-only FLEX rows using oxygen, log initial diameter and log pressure; it reports AUC 0.884 from five shuffled stratified row folds and 200 bootstrap fits. Our independent source-CSV eligibility recount matches its **94 rows: 54 Extinction, 27 Disruption, 13 Completion; 53 methanol and 41 heptane**. Only literal Extinction is positive; disrupted tests join completion as negatives. No fuel term separates the two fuels. This can evaluate the recorded label discrimination within that pooled cohort; it does not by itself identify whether a disrupted droplet would naturally extinguish before eventual burnout. Row-fold validation is not intrinsically invalid—the endpoint, dependency and deployment question determine what it supports. [Fit][H-fit], [published coefficients/score][H-model].

The card correctly identifies droplets, flags oxygen extrapolation and states when pressure is clamped. The clamp uses nominal 0.7–1.0 atm while our eligible-row pressure recount is approximately 0.703–1.019 atm; nominal and measured ranges should be distinguished. Its habitat headline still evaluates a fixed 3 mm droplet at a potentially different clamped pressure, not the requested physical atmosphere. [Extinction engine][H-extinction], [card][H-extinction-card]. NASA's report distinguishes disruptive outcomes and both radiative and diffusive extinction; these are not interchangeable labels. [NASA TP-2015-216046, PDF pages 8 and 22–23][N-flex].

### MicroFire-V2: narrow admission, unusually explicit support rules

The admitted catalog is one BASS-II B20 record. Its known values have locators; unextracted gravity and unreported per-run pressure remain unknown. Ranking uses retrieval tolerances, but direct observed support requires an exact admitted statement and exact recorded requested conditions. It explicitly says absent catalog evidence is not evidence of global absence. Quarantined IDs cannot enter the validated catalog. These are substantive features, not merely disclaimers. [Catalog][F-catalog], [evidence][F-evidence], [quarantine][F-quarantine].

We visually inspected the already acquired original **Table A.1, PDF page 112 / printed page 104**, whose hash matches MicroFire's catalog source hash. In the same B20 row (as-run 50), both calibrated initial/final oxygen columns read 17.4 vol%; the adjacent uncalibrated initial/final columns, headed initial/final O2 without the calibrated qualifier, both read 16.5. This is a column distinction, not an adjacent-row error. The flow entry mixes 5, 3 and a potentiometer setting, and the observation states full-sample burning. The catalog's correction is supported. A generic statement of oxygen without its column/basis needs clarification before being declared wrong. No claim of lunar transfer follows. [NASA BASS-II report][N-bass]; local `primary_inspection/`.

Its 11 intelligence cases and five citation checks are explicitly author-written source-specific regressions without independent scientific acceptance. Its earlier predictive classifier is explicitly **not deployed** because of quarantined inputs, limited class balance and unestablished gain. The comparison UI also labels its toy readouts illustrative. The limitation is coverage and generalization: one admitted record cannot establish meaningful cross-study retrieval quality or physical response prediction. IGNIS should learn from this admission discipline without mistaking a larger metadata count for stronger scientific validation. [Evaluation][F-eval], [nondeployment decision][F-model-decision], [compare UI][F-compare].

### IGNIS LAB: actual modeling work beyond current IGNIS

The inspected training code uses only pre-ignition inputs; excludes Disruption from the extinction/completion target; keeps repeated nominal condition groups together; includes per-fuel inputs/interactions; and compares against base-rate and per-fuel-mean baselines. Saved fold records and the search report report approximately **0.89 AUC / 0.074 Brier** for extinction and **0.045 mm²/s MAE** for K. The model search additionally describes a campaign stress test removing training rows that share a held-out campaign's condition. These are stronger predictive-evaluation artifacts than IGNIS currently has, although no scores or pre-registration chronology were independently rerun/verified here. [Cleaning][L-clean], [training][L-train], [fold results][L-folds], [search report][L-search].

Its raw-history feature fits d² against time over a stated 20–100% burn-time window, flags low fit R², shows a worst discrepancy, and discloses that the downloaded histories are the first 30 of 260, not a random sample. The project reports a 2.3% median K discrepancy for 15 comparable droplets. This is a useful source-data/refit check, not independent proof of prediction accuracy or all-history agreement. [Raw-history implementation][L-droplets], [d² page][L-d2], [reported result][L-readme].

The evidence gate checks per-fuel ranges and nearest-neighbor distance, includes nearby recorded outcomes, and downgrades regions dominated by Disruption. Credit this joint-condition support check, which is more informative than marginal min/max alone. The RED UI withholds headline numbers but has a clearly warned raw-output expander; “no output anywhere outside support” would overstate its abstention. [Evidence gate][L-evidence], [prediction page, lines 60–100][L-predict].

**Scientific limits:** only 27 Completion outcomes underpin the classifier; high pressure and CO2 co-occur; model selection and evaluations remain within FLEX; grouped split-conformal calibration pools per-row residuals from held-out groups. The reported empirical interval coverage is useful, but a distribution-free guarantee for new campaigns or materials needs an explicit exchangeability/unit-of-calibration argument. These are scope and validation requirements, not evidence that the reported grouped gains are fabricated. [Calibration][L-calibration], [model-search limitations][L-search]. Current IGNIS must describe its model-assessment workflow separately from a fitted predictor.

### Kenyalang Blue Flame: broader gravity/planning, with specific source-to-claim defects

The literature layer has **24 entries from seven sources**, comprising 15 limit values, two observed-O2 entries, three spread-rate entries and four qualitative entries. It distinguishes preliminary posters, source platforms, material, flow and pressure, and warns against combining different limit definitions. This is a real breadth advantage over IGNIS's current interactive data. It includes nine Saffire literature cases, with version-specific earlier conditions and later support, rather than using IGNIS's single-version canonical table. Differences in those values should not automatically be called transcription errors. [Partial-g data][K-partial], [partial-g builder][K-partial-builder], [Saffire records][K-saffire].

Its planner constructs intersections of habitat atmosphere windows and greedily covers habitat × fuel-class gaps, yielding the saved **nine planned conditions / 15 gaps**. Source code explicitly excludes gravity from the coverage guarantee and describes microgravity's limits for Moon/Mars. Useful planning UX, but this is not a minimum validated scientific experiment program: the greedy rule does not prove global optimality, a fuel class is not a material/protocol cohort, and successful window coverage does not close a physical-transfer gap. Its 65 data-health flags are candidates for curator review, not 65 independently confirmed NASA errors. [Planner, lines 176–212 and 300–306][K-planner], [saved plan][K-plan], [health rules][K-health].

Three concrete corrections are warranted:

1. **Diluent domain:** the model trains on 273 labelled FLEX rows across diluents and builds the per-fuel oxygen/pressure hull from all of them, while the published method/curve describe N2 balance. The features omit diluent and initial droplet diameter. An O2/pressure hull supported partly by helium/CO2 conditions does not establish N2-specific support. The JSON candidly exposes `hull_uses_all_diluents`; that disclosure does not make the conditional prediction N2-specific. A useful fix is either a documented pooled-population target or N2-specific fitting/eligibility, with the query covariates represented. [Fit, lines 47–75, 164–170, 454–498][K-fit], [model artifact][K-model].
2. **Endpoint name:** literal Extinction is labeled “radiative extinction,” although the NASA report states that both radiative and diffusive extinction occurred. Use a source-faithful recorded endpoint unless an independently sourced mechanism label is available per row. Its explicit mixed Disruption/Completion negative-class note is a strength, but does not resolve this positive-class labeling issue. [Fit, lines 28 and 455–459][K-fit], [NASA TP, PDF page 8][N-flex].
3. **Martian evidence contradiction:** selecting Mars reaches UI text saying no NASA partial-g Mars data exist in these sources (`public/app.js:2478`), while its own literature file has three Olson/Ferkul Martian entries and the chart says lunar and Martian. Generate the card from the admitted source inventory instead of leaving an earlier absence claim. [UI][K-ui], [partial-g records][K-partial].

Its reported ten-fold date-group evaluation, 80 dates, AUC **0.847**, Brier **0.1441** versus baseline **0.2231**, and cluster-bootstrap intervals deserve credit as evaluation of its specified mixed endpoint. Those results were not independently rerun and are not directly comparable to HALO's N2-only or IGNIS LAB's Extinction-versus-Completion tasks. [Saved model validation][K-model].

## 4. Correct the competitive story before adding features

The acquired PSI-table **22.2% maximum** is a bounded statement about selected solid-material rows with recorded oxygen. MARGIN and HALO retain PSI qualifiers; Kenyalang also adds outside-PSI Saffire cases. Do not accuse them of making a global “NASA never burned solids above 22.2%” claim. Conversely, their prose calling all 2,009 table records “tests” should not be copied: daily aggregate rows, smoke records, maintenance entries and repeated records have different units of observation. A nominal/assumed cabin pressure also cannot prove that every source run had a measured pressure of 1 atm. [MARGIN counts][M-snapshot], [HALO README][H-readme], [Kenyalang README][K-readme].

A scalar proximity/relevance score can be an honest discovery aid when its axes, scales, missingness and intended use are visible. The scientific objection is interpreting that score as physical equivalence, safety, or a validated transfer prediction. Equally, a Pareto frontier does not fix incorrect eligibility or supply missing transport, material-history, radiation or chemistry information. IGNIS's frontier should be sold as a transparent bounded comparison operation.

**Recommended priorities, in order:**

1. **Demonstrate the existing audit path.** Use one condition cohort, one dominated-case witness, and Kapton's 20/35 below-cutoff result with its missing cell and separate identities. Then show an incompatible requested model quantity. This distinguishes a useful operation without pretending to match competitors' predictive breadth.
2. **Measure researcher benefit.** Freeze a small task set before UI tuning: locate a case, recover its source version, identify a missing denominator cell, and refuse an incompatible comparison. Compare IGNIS against a curated spreadsheet with blinded answer scoring and time/error measures. Independent source review precedes the study; a faster wrong answer is failure.
3. **Review a bounded partial-g extension and exclusion ledger.** Start with primary-source limit definitions and the 2021 Saffire flow table. Admit each platform, material, geometry, endpoint and source version separately. Do not simply import competitors' curated numbers or transform a literature range into a run measurement. This follows a demonstrated user need, not a novelty claim.
4. **Treat predictive modeling as a later validated module.** If undertaken, freeze outcome/censoring semantics, dilution, fuel, diameter and grouping; compare to simple baselines; preserve a prospective independent dataset. The present W1/W2/W3 infrastructure can make those requirements explicit. It cannot substitute for that evaluation.

Falsifiers: the proposed IGNIS advantage fails if users cannot reconstruct the displayed denominator/source from an export; if a hidden missing axis makes a comparison appear stronger; if same-cohort status is read as physical transfer; or if blinded task performance does not improve over a spreadsheet. No result from this review justifies a quantum module: chemistry matters to the eventual reaction/transport model and endpoint definitions, not to these current source-curation and comparison operations.

## 5. Access, provenance and reproducibility

All new captures and scripts are under `_raw/competitive_deep_2026-11-15/physics/`. `acquisition.jsonl` records URLs, UTC start/end, status, body bytes, SHA-256, TLS verification and absence of authentication. At the completed capture pass: **154 requests logged; 151 successful unique body paths; three initial sandbox network failures; zero successful-body hash mismatches**. The initial selected-file batch also stopped on a local containment assertion; the missing paths were bounded and retried, documented in `capture_notes.json`. No source was executed.

Read-only homepage GETs returned HTTP 200 for [MARGIN](https://margin.adai.systems/), its previous Vercel host, [HALO](https://halo-fire-brief.vercel.app/), [MicroFire-V2](https://microfire-v2-preview.vercel.app/), and [Kenyalang](https://flame-in-freefall-three.vercel.app/). The MARGIN bodies matched byte-for-byte; the first three contained server-rendered product text. Kenyalang returned its static loading shell. No JavaScript interaction, backend, inference, visual correctness or live deployment-to-commit match was verified. IGNIS LAB's metadata gave no public homepage; no guessed deployment was probed.

The reproducible local audit uses `inspect_saved_evidence.py` for descriptive recounts, page-body summaries, hashes and current IGNIS file pins. `inspect_primary_pages.py` records existing NASA PDF hashes/pages and renders B20 for direct inspection. The source-capture scripts are `capture_public.py` and `capture_additional.py`; they perform public bounded GETs only. Raw captures support internal review and do not imply permission to republish entire reports, screenshots or third-party code under IGNIS's license. Public availability, NASA hosting, PSI record licensing and repository code licensing are separate questions.

**Contribution to carry forward:** a reproducible evidence-exploration and model-comparison workflow that keeps source versions, cells, missingness and requested quantities attached to the result. **Still unknown:** independent source-review acceptance, comparative user utility, competitor runtime correctness, external model generalization, and actual judging/submission outcomes.
