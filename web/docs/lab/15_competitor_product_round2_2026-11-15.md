# Product workflow comparison, second public review — 15 November 2026

This review compares actual research workflows with **IGNIS web-2026-11-15.3**, registry `2026-11-15.r2`. It supersedes the product-gap conclusions of the earlier build2 comparison, not its immutable observations. IGNIS now has paired detector analysis, exact selected-analysis handoff, portable notebook recipes and receipts, import/recomputation with drift reporting, and Data & methods. It is inaccurate to describe these as integration-pending or to claim that receipt replay alone differentiates another product from current IGNIS.

The strongest useful comparisons are specific: VESTA packages coverage with evidence and rechecks it; MORS adds selectable, source-row FLEX plots; MicroFire Atlas publishes a usable hypothetical research-planning artifact; FlameBound makes scenarios easy to share; ZERO FLARE organizes saved evidence around a research shelf; MicroFire V2 treats a learner's notes separately from admitted source lineage. These are distinct strengths, not a single ranking of scientific quality.

Recheck distinguishes method, basis and result/evidence changes; UI application is gated on a matching recheck. That is useful reproducibility machinery, with an explicit warning that rechecking is not scientific validation. IGNIS now offers the corresponding research-notebook capability and must compare the contents of receipts, not whether a Save button exists. A separately fetched [deployed evidence module](https://itzjk.github.io/vesta/js/world/act-evidence.js) is **not byte-identical** to the pinned module: it adds selected material/concern synchronization and receipt-question preview. Both byte hashes are retained. This may be a subsequent release; it is not proof of a broken deployment or the identity of its full source revision. The public design view was observed, and the receipt action was visible. VESTA's README also explicitly separates MIT code/authored documentation, PSI data, NASA quotations and font rights. ## Concrete priorities for IGNIS build3

1. **Share the selected research question.** Add a small, versioned URL contract for recognized view, condition and detector selections. Validate restored values, identify the current registry/method context, and say when a link restores selections rather than original evidence. Keep original result/source cells in assessment JSON and full recipe recheck in the notebook. This is the most direct demonstrated convenience gap against FlameBound and VESTA.
2. **Offer a few guided task starts using existing controls.** Useful entries are “compare these seven reported conditions,” “audit paired-channel missingness and flight strata,” and “revisit a claim after adding source context.” Each should populate the real workspace, retain the exact selection through assessment/export, and expose source rows. This improves discoverability without inventing a new data model or copying a broad mission game.
3. **Explain the three handoff choices at the point of use.** Share view; save/import/recheck a notebook recipe; export an assessment with original results and source cells. Current IGNIS implements the latter two, but their different purposes should be immediately visible. Checksums remain integrity checks, not independent review or authenticity signatures.

Atlas's next-question board and MORS's measurement studio are credible later reference designs. A compact IGNIS next-admission checklist can already use the four reviewed data opportunities and actual unresolved comparison fields. It should not manufacture NASA experiment priority, information gain, physical risk or safety classification. Cutoff robustness belongs to the existing descriptive paired-data contract, with explicit denominators and source/configuration caveats; it is not detector alarm prediction.

Accessibility evidence here is limited to inspected labels, focusable controls and observed page behavior. It does not establish WCAG conformance. No dead button was established by clicking: implementation handlers and stale copy were inspected, and inaccessible dependencies under the review's API block were not counted as product defects. Live AI quality, broad statistical validation, deployment readiness and NASA adoption remain unestablished for this comparison. IGNIS's live interpretation remains NOT RUN/disabled, with publication and original-code licensing still owner decisions.
