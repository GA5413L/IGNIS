Adding an AUC or a surrogate without those foundations would not provide that capability.

No model was fitted, no inference provider was called, and no frozen IE-1 packet, questions, scoring, task split or credentials were changed. ## A narrow ML candidate, and why it does not pass the fitting gate yet

The most defensible candidate available here is **prediction of the recorded FLEX termination category under a specified historical protocol**, using only pre-test information. A small regularized multinomial model for Extinction/Disruption/Completion would preserve more endpoint meaning than calling all non-extinction rows sustained burning. A binary visible-extinction model could be studied only with an explicit alternative-label policy and sensitivity analysis. Neither is a safe-atmosphere predictor, a causal oxygen effect, or a general microgravity flammability law.

For a time-to-extinction target, a protocol ending without extinction can be censored only after establishing that extinction had not occurred by the observation end and what the stop time means. Disruption may be a competing event or informative termination; it must not automatically become an independent right-censored observation. Completion is also not an infinite extinction time. The software authors' competing-risk documentation distinguishes non-informative censoring from events that prevent the target event. Applying that framework to FLEX requires original run/protocol adjudication, not a relabeling of `Test end`. [scikit-survival competing risks](https://scikit-survival.readthedocs.io/en/stable/user_guide/competing-risks.html).

| Requirement before fitting | Current status / acceptance condition |
|---|---|
| Dataset and unit contract | Reconcile the 274-row local table with other artifacts; retain original units and locators; acquire definitions for ambiguous fields. Each model-ready row must link to an actual test and source version. |
| Target and prediction time | Define visible-flame endpoint, observation window and intended use; distinguish termination cause from extinction time. Audit all labels; no inferred safe/unsafe target. |
| Features and leakage | Pre-test oxygen fraction, total pressure, diluent composition, fuel and initial size only if available at prediction time. Exclude extinction diameter, burn duration/rate, final atmosphere and any outcome-conditioned value. Fit preprocessing, feature selection, class weights, calibration and decision thresholds using training information only. |
| Dependence and sampling | Identify campaign/apparatus/calibration, atmosphere preparation, repeated protocol and run ancestry. Hold out the unit matching intended generalization. Date/atmosphere group checks are sensitivity analyses if full ancestry is unresolved. All rows from the same dependency group must remain together. |
| Evaluation | Constant class-proportion baseline fitted on training groups, a small regularized model, and an explicit missingness-only/metadata-only diagnostic baseline. Use nested group-aware selection; separate calibration and final assessment. Report each fuel and held-out domain, not just pooled AUC. |
| Scientific value | Predeclare a meaningful improvement on a specified task, its costs and abstention policy. Require better out-of-group proper-score performance with useful calibration and adequate subgroup support; not a single favorable AUC. No unsupported extrapolation or physical deployment from internal CV. |

Grouped CV prevents a supplied group appearing in both train and test; it cannot repair an incorrect grouping definition or establish a new-population target. An unknown fuel cannot be represented as a familiar categorical level, and a two-fuel pooled leave-one-fuel-out score does not establish broad fuel transfer. [Official grouped-CV documentation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/cross_validation.html#cross-validation-iterators-for-grouped-data).

Calibration has to be tested separately from ranking. Calibration data must be disjoint from classifier fitting data, with the dependence structure respected; threshold tuning also needs its own evaluation. Report reliability curves with bin counts and held-out log/Brier scores, without claiming Brier alone measures calibration: it combines reliability, resolution and outcome uncertainty. With only a few hundred correlated rows, a flexible recalibration curve is not automatically justified. [Official calibration documentation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/calibration.html).

Prediction intervals also require assumptions. Conformal methods do not automatically repair dependent campaigns, adaptive experiment selection or an unseen fuel. Barber et al. quantify consequences of exchangeability violations and develop qualified extensions; an unconditional coverage label is not a guarantee for every material or regime. For physical parameters, simulation discrepancy can be confounded with calibration parameters even when a fit looks good. A sensor inversion needs its measurement/transport/calibration model, not just another learner. [Barber et al., author version v5, pp.1–2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13415v5), [Brynjarsdóttir and O'Hagan, author manuscript, pp.1–3](https://www.tonyohagan.co.uk/academic/pdf/simmach.pdf).

**No-go is about readiness and authorized scope, not a finding that ML cannot help.** A later source-extraction or document-classification assistant is a different, nonphysical task: it can earn value by reducing curator correction time while preserving exact field/quote/unit binding and unknowns against deterministic extraction/retrieval baselines. It would need human-adjudicated task labels and evaluation; it does not license a physical predictor or alter the frozen interpretation study.

## New useful finding without ML

The audit pairs **AW: ISS detector scatter** with **AV: ISS obscuration**, solely on the same workbook/sheet/row. It applies two separately declared illustrative cutoffs, 2 V for A and 2 V for B. Both are stored voltages with different measurement operators; equal numerical cutoffs do not make their response scales equivalent. This is neither alarm classification nor a comparison of detector quality.

DBP references are excluded. `0` remains finite; blank/error/non-numeric cells are unavailable. On 134 selected rows, 133 are jointly finite:

| Same-row state | Count |
|---|---:|
| A below, B below | 32 |
| A below, B at/above | 6 |
| A at/above, B below | 76 |
| A at/above, B at/above | 19 |
| Both unavailable | 1 |

The difference between the two at/above fractions on paired rows is `(76−6)/133 = 70/133`. Its sign changes with flight selection: **flight 1: −6/25; flight 2: +76/108**. Every finite flight-1 AV reading is at/above the illustrative cutoff; every finite flight-2 AV reading is below it. This is a prompt to inspect configuration, offset, processing and measurement window. The new-source specialist did not establish an AV calibration change from the inspected SAME-R report. The pattern itself cannot supply that explanation.

A second exact sensitivity exposes a tempting cleaning error. All 25 selected flight-1 rows have the literal source token `n` in `fanProblem`; all 109 selected flight-2 rows have it unrecorded. Requiring recorded `n` therefore selects only flight 1 and changes the pooled difference's sign. Unknown fan status is not evidence of failure or success. The audit exports all 109 excluded row IDs and original flag cells, making the population change reviewable.

Missing-value sensitivity is small for this particular comparison, while cohort sensitivity is large. The unrecorded pair is **AW50/AV50**, sample K02, Test ID 4K, SAME #51a. Under unrestricted binary completions at the declared cutoffs, the full 134-row difference lies in **[69,71]/134**. For Kapton alone, 36 selected/35 paired rows give `9/35`, with full-selection bounds **[8,10]/36**. These are exact finite-data bounds under the stated completion policy, not imputed voltages, confidence intervals or physical predictions. They do not account for calibration error in recorded readings.

The separate [data-opportunity report](12_data_opportunities_2026-11-15.md) inspects new SAME-R ancestry and NIST ground-test alarm data. Those results must remain source/configuration specific: a source report's 109 listed observations cannot be equated to this workbook's 109 non-DBP flight-2 rows without a crosswalk. A ground alarm/obscuration calibration cannot be transferred to SAME voltages solely because both concern smoke.

## Exact implementable contract: paired-channel sensitivity

The research script implements the fixed AW/AV example and strata above. A subsequently authorized pure application core now implements the operation in `web/src/core/pairedDetector.ts`, with meaningful tests in `web/tests/unit/pairedDetector.test.ts`. The coordinator owns application/UI integration and execution of the full checks; those checks were pending at this report update. The core accepts numeric `cutoffA`/`cutoffB` in volts, uses the current pinned detector dataset, and also supports the explicit optional literal-recorded-n fan filter. The following fuller contract records the scientific provenance requirement for a saved analysis; session attachments should bind its dataset hash.

```ts
pairedChannelSensitivity({
  datasetSha256,
  flight: 'all' | '1' | '2',
  material: 'all' | DetectorMaterial,
  includeReference: boolean,
  channelA: DetectorChannelId,
  cutoffA: { value: number, unit: 'V' },
  channelB: DetectorChannelId,
  cutoffB: { value: number, unit: 'V' },
  exclusions: Array<{ recordId: string, reason: string }>
})
```

1. Validate the exact input schema, known dataset hash/enums, two distinct channels, finite nonnegative cutoffs, unique exclusion IDs belonging to the selected base cohort and nonempty reasons. Reject unknown fields/IDs and nonfinite values. Never join by a displayed sample or test label.
2. Pair by `(workbookSha256, sheet, excelRow)` only. Select the cohort, then apply explicit exclusions; retain each excluded source record and reason. Recorded use/status tokens are attached, not converted into unstated operation flags. Do not recalculate workbook formulas.
3. Classify each finite channel as below or at/above its own cutoff. Return its own finite denominator and each unavailable status. On jointly finite rows return the four-cell table, exact numerator/denominator for the paired difference, and row/cell witnesses. No paired value is substituted from another row.
4. Return the same summaries by flight and material plus each selected exclusion variant. Empty denominators produce `null`, never zero or NaN. Sort by stable source row identity for deterministic JSON; include no timestamps in the analysis body.
5. For full-selection bounds, a known binary status has interval `[s,s]`; an unavailable one has `[0,1]`. Sum `lowerA−upperB` and `upperA−lowerB` over all selected rows and divide by the unchanged selected-row count. Marginal at/above bounds are `[knownAbove, knownAbove+unavailable]/N`. These endpoints are attainable under unrestricted binary completions; no independence or missing-at-random premise is required.
6. Export `schemaVersion`, input/data hash, source metadata/operator definitions, selection and exclusions, marginal statuses, joint table, exact rational counts, strata, bounds, rows, identity conflicts and review/claim limitations. A saved session must replay the source snapshot and policy, not silently substitute current data.

Meaningful checks: partition counts sum to selected rows; paired counts sum to the paired denominator; row/cell identities agree; zero is retained; equality goes to at/above; missing never becomes zero; unknown exclusions fail; empty strata remain null; permutation preserves summaries; swapping A/B transposes the table and negates/reverses difference bounds. No p-value or binomial confidence interval is justified by treating these dependent source rows as independent trials.

## Experimental design and fronts: where ML could add value later

An experimental-design method needs an explicit decision utility, candidate measurements, costs, constraints and a model of what each observation would reveal. The current Huan–Jagalur–Marzouk author abstract describes this distinction between choosing a design criterion and computing/optimizing it; it is not a ready-made fire experiment planner. [Author abstract, revised 29 April 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16212v2).

Multiobjective Bayesian optimization adds a probabilistic surrogate and an acquisition criterion such as expected hypervolume improvement, with declared objectives and a reference point. It is different from an exact frontier of already observed condition gaps. Neither a GP's internal variance nor a nondominated point proves physical usefulness. No such surrogate is fitted here. [Daulton et al., NeurIPS 2020, §§2–3](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/hash/6fec24eac8f18ed793f5eaad3dd7977c-Abstract.html).

A useful immediate alternative is a **dependency-aware model-use audit**: source datum → its role in initialization, calibration, normalization, model selection or evaluation → the conclusion affected by removing that datum. Preserve the T1–T4 operator/window contracts. It can identify when an apparent agreement was imposed, which later response comparison remains informative, and which new measurement would resolve a specific ambiguity. Keep a coverage gap or proposed measurement as a hypothesis/requirement, not a quantified expected discovery. NASA-STD-7009B explicitly ties credibility and use assessment to intended use and validation domain; it does not confer a global model-quality badge. [Approved NASA-STD-7009B, §§4.1–4.3](https://standards.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/standards/NASA/B/1/NASA-STD-7009B-Final-3-5-2024.pdf).
