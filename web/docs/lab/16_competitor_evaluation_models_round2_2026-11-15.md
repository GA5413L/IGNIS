## Exact cutoff-range certificate: derivation and actual-data witness

Use the existing same-workbook/sheet/row pairing. Freeze cohort and exclusions before comparing cutoffs. Let S be the N jointly finite rows and M the full selected population. For independent analyst cutoffs a,b define

`D(a,b) = (sum[i in S] 1[A_i >= a] - sum[i in S] 1[B_i >= b]) / N`.

Each A indicator is nonincreasing in a; each negated B indicator is nondecreasing in b. Therefore, on the closed rectangle `[aL,aU] × [bL,bU]`, the exact minimum is `D(aU,bL)` and maximum is `D(aL,bU)`. Endpoints are attained. For N=0 report no paired fraction. This elementary proof, not a grid or fitted approximation, establishes the range certificate.

For full-selection counts let KA(a), KB(b) count known at-or-above readings and mA,mB count unavailable channels. Every unrestricted binary completion has difference in

`[(KA-KB-mB)/M, (KA-KB+mA)/M]`.

Both endpoints are attainable by assigning the unknown binary statuses accordingly. The same two corners bound the entire rectangle. These are finite completion bounds under unrestricted missing statuses; they are not imputed voltage values, confidence intervals, independent-trial estimates or probabilities of future outcomes. No independence assumption among existing rows is needed for these counts. Additional physical constraints on missing readings could narrow the set only if separately justified.

The empirical chart also has an exact partition. For a closed axis `[L,U]`, emit `{L}`, then intervals `(previous,endpoint]` where endpoints are sorted distinct observed values in `(L,U]`, plus U. Evaluate each interval at its upper endpoint. With `>=`, an observation remains counted at its own cutoff and drops just after it; arbitrary epsilon probes can be wrong. Degenerate ranges have one cell. Ties create one combined drop. In conventional right-continuous ECDF notation the at-or-above fraction is `1-F(t-)`, not `1-F(t)` at an observed tie. The staircase basis is supported by [SciPy's ECDF documentation](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.ecdf.html) and [NIST's empirical-CDF description](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/software/dataplot/refman1/auxillar/ecdfplot.htm); their inferential interval procedures are not used. Both full HTML sources were acquired. Manski's missing-data partial-identification paper was available only as a publisher abstract; an attempted author PDF returned404, so no equation was transcribed from it. [Publisher abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0888613X04001124).
