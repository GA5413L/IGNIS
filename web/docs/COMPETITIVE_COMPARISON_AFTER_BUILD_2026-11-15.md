# IGNIS compared with current public competitors

15 November 2026, PKT. **IGNIS is a credible evidence-analysis prototype, but the available evidence does not establish that it leads the strongest public projects overall.** Its useful strengths are source-cell inspection, explicit measurement limits, compatible condition cohorts and preservation of changed model-use claims. Several competitors are ahead in scenario sharing, reproducible analysis replay, guided demonstrations, predictive-model evaluation and published AI evaluation artifacts.

The strongest improvement strategy is to complete and evaluate one precise research workflow. Adding more profile cards, a generic chatbot or an unvalidated fire simulation would not distinguish IGNIS from this field.

This comparison supersedes the current-build statements in the [earlier competitive snapshot](COMPETITIVE_UPDATE_2026-11-15.md), which preceded application integration. That historical report remains unchanged. The present baseline is implemented build `web-2026-11-15.2`, registry `2026-11-15.r2`, rather than the proposed design.

## Scope and confidence

Fresh GitHub searches returned **243 distinct repositories**, not 243 verified competing teams. The exact challenge-phrase query returned 178 indexed matches across both pages; broader queries included unrelated work and one capped result set. We refreshed 22 repository heads and inspected selected implementation/evaluation files in 17 relevant repositories. MicroFire Atlas and MicroFire V2 share an owner; another public product uses the name Emberfall and is distinct from our registered team.

The comparison combines pinned source inspection, reading and recounting published evaluation artifacts, limited public-page inspection, and fresh read-only checks of the current IGNIS server. It does not install competitors' software, rerun their predictive models, submit AI questions, or establish a common blinded benchmark. Deployed pages may differ from the inspected source commits. Reported model metrics below retain their original tasks, denominators and evaluation limitations.

The source audits and acquisition records are linked in the [physics review](lab/07_competitive_physics_2026-11-15.md), [product review](lab/08_competitive_product_2026-11-15.md), [AI and evaluation review](lab/09_competitive_ai_evaluation_2026-11-15.md), and [additional competitors and IGNIS review](lab/10_competitive_additional_2026-11-15.md). ## What IGNIS actually does well

**The detector example supports a concrete, reviewable result.** Selecting Kapton, ISS scatter and a descriptive 2 V cutoff returns 36 rows, 35 finite readings and 20 below the cutoff, with the blank AW50 retained. The 20/35 fraction is not an alarm-miss rate. SAME number and Test ID remain separate, and an unresolved CSV/workbook identity conflict is visible rather than silently joined. HALO independently implements the same below-cutoff counts; IGNIS's value is the full measurement and identity trail, not discovery of a new percentage. [Reproduction instructions](RESEARCH_REUSE.md), [detector code](../src/core/detector.ts), [physics comparison](lab/07_competitive_physics_2026-11-15.md).

**Our condition comparison exposes a tradeoff without disguising it as a physical probability.** The seven Saffire cases are separated into four material/burning-side cohorts. Oxygen and pressure gaps remain separate; the finite Pareto set answers a stated coordinate question. Flow is excluded because the retained publications conflict. This avoids some arbitrary score aggregation, but cohort choice, variables, source versions and unmeasured conditions still matter. It is a condition-gap frontier, not evidence that IGNIS lies on a system-performance or fire-safety Pareto frontier. MARGIN has the same seven revised cases and additional source reconciliation. [Conditions implementation](../src/core/conditions.ts), [MARGIN audit](lab/07_competitive_physics_2026-11-15.md).

**The model-assessment revision has a defensible purpose.** A later CO₂-response comparison, source-total normalization and chemical-heat-release question require different measurement/claim contracts even when the named model remains unchanged. IGNIS preserves the prior assessment and the reason its conclusion changed, with matching Markdown/JSON exports. This is a promising specialist workflow, not a claim that other projects have no revision or replay features. [Revision implementation](../src/core/revisions.ts), [export implementation](../src/core/export.ts), [judge walkthrough](JUDGE_WALKTHROUGH_2026-11-15.md).

The implemented UI is readable and uses source-based plots. Its existing local checks passed **243 offline tests and 17 browser cases, with one intentional duplicate skip**. Those tests support software behavior, not 100% scientific correctness, competitive superiority or independent validation. Fresh health/evidence GETs confirmed the current build was running with zero registry errors. [Verification record](LOCAL_VERIFICATION_2026-11-15.md), [fresh health capture](../../_raw/competitive_deep_2026-11-15/ignis/health.json).

## Where IGNIS is behind

1. **The selected analysis does not yet flow into the brief.** The Conditions and Smoke lab buttons prepare a pillar preset; they do not carry the selected cases/readings into the assessed evidence. Those selections have separate exports. This is the most important gap between the connected-workspace proposition and actual behavior. [Workspace](../src/components/Workspace.tsx), [Conditions](../src/components/Conditions.tsx), [Smoke lab](../src/components/DetectorLab.tsx).
2. **A colleague cannot yet reopen the exact workspace.** Revision history is in browser memory. There is no implemented full save/import/replay or scenario-link workflow in the inspected application. VESTA, MORS and FlameBound set a higher standard here. [Product audit](lab/08_competitive_product_2026-11-15.md), [FlameBound URL code](https://github.com/prattaybanik03/flamebound-flame-in-freefall/blob/a7adbbeb05f3c4cd6af3b7141c91e8ed09d8d9ac/web/src/lib/url.ts).
3. **Live interpretation is unavailable.** Health says `aiAvailable: false`, with no accepted configuration. The challenge explicitly asks for an SI-powered dashboard; the deterministic workflow is useful, but this is a material presentation/relevance gap. Keys are being prepared, and evaluation/activation gates remain unchanged. [Health capture](../../_raw/competitive_deep_2026-11-15/ignis/health.json), [provided challenge text](../../DOCS/For%20decades,%20NASA%20has%20studied%20how%20f.txt).
4. **Evaluation of researcher benefit is missing.** We have no independent reviewer, blinded comparison to an equally informed spreadsheet, measured source-checking effort or current live interpretation result. Competitor artifacts are imperfect, but several provide more outcome evidence than our test count. [Evaluation review](lab/09_competitive_ai_evaluation_2026-11-15.md).
5. **Public reuse is unfinished.** The original-code license is undecided and publication remains held. A local evidence API and reproducibility guide help; researchers still need a stable, versioned, accessible release and meaningful tasks they can repeat. Several reviewed competitors already supply public source licenses and hosted demonstrations. [Reuse status](RESEARCH_REUSE.md).

The atlas's **24 profiles and 2,009 nonblank table records** must not be presented as 24 deeply reviewed findings or 2,009 independent burns. The core assessment registry contains 16 findings; source profiles, literature cases and workbook measurements have distinct depth and provenance. Larger competitor row counts require the same scrutiny.

The field does not support a universal API-provider choice. StarryNight's inspected configuration uses Groq for narration; other projects expose Gemini, Claude, OpenAI-compatible or deterministic alternatives. A configured provider is not proof of a funded live deployment, and a published run on another task does not choose IGNIS's interpreter. Keep the existing bounded evaluation and frozen scoring criteria; this comparison authorizes no additional inference.

## The next improvements with the highest value

| Priority | Concrete result to build or measure | Completion evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Carry the selected evidence through | A selected detector/condition analysis becomes a typed, source-bound part of its brief, with limits and identities retained. Preserve the distinction from evidence admitted for model validation. | Change the selection, prepare the brief, and inspect matching source IDs, denominator, units, conflict status and source version in both screen and export. |
| 2. Make a research session reproducible | Export/import the exact request, selections, source versions and assessment history; detect stale or changed evidence. A stable shareable scenario can supplement this. | Reopen in a clean browser and recover the same analysis; report a changed source/version instead of silently reusing a conclusion. |
| 3. Complete the bounded interpreter evaluation | Use only the locally designated credential and authorized public packet under existing gates. Accept the cheapest configuration that passes the frozen task criteria, rather than copying a competitor's model. | Actual interpretation errors, schema failures, refusals, latency and conservatively accounted usage; unavailable runs remain NOT RUN. |
| 4. Demonstrate benefit on equal evidence | Compare IGNIS with a curated spreadsheet supplied with the same NASA information and then with the strongest relevant competitor workflow. | Source-correct task answers, harmful false admissions, completion time and revision/source-checking effort. Preserve failures and distinguish assistant checks from independent participants. |
| 5. Make the story and release reviewable | One short journey: question → measurement → unresolved condition/identity → bounded answer → changed question → reproducible export. Finish stable release identity and licensing when the owner resolves publication items. | Another person can follow the demonstration and reproduce the result from a versioned artifact. No public release occurs during this research task. |

The first two address shortcomings already visible in our own implementation. The third addresses the missing live SI path. These take precedence over increasing catalogue size or adding a speculative simulator.

This task set is proposed, **NOT RUN as a common benchmark**. If no independent participant is available before submission, report an assistant-performed protocol demonstration with that exact label. Do not call it independent validation or general superiority.

## Competition fit and research adoption

The supplied official judging guide evaluates Impact, Creativity, Validity, Relevance and Presentation; it does not prescribe a minimum record count or require a newly trained physical model. A meaningful improvement can support creativity, but a generic evidence browser is now a crowded approach. Our current implementation is strongest on inspectable validity and weakest on measured impact, live SI and frictionless handoff. This mapping is our interpretation of the rubric. [Official judging guide](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/judging-awards-guide/), [saved guide used for this review](../../DOCS/NASA%20Space%20Apps%20Challenge2.pdf). The official page returned 502 during the fresh lookup, so the criteria were checked against the supplied local copy.

For researcher or NASA reuse, the plausible contribution is a small, reproducible evidence/measurement package and useful software contract, followed by independent review and maintenance. Current source conflicts can be documented without claiming NASA made an error. An award, source audit or demonstration would not establish adoption. No adoption, external endorsement, physical SOTA result or measured overall competitor lead is established by this research.
