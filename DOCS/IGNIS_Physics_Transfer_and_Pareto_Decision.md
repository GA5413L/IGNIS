# IGNIS — physics-grounded evidence transfer, revision and a credible frontier target

**Research date:** 14 November 2026  
**Project:** IGNIS / Emberfall  
**Status:** research/design refinement of the existing project; no application, physical-model replay, new experiment, API inference or external review performed.

## 1. Decision: deepen one operation, not the feature list

Retain the previous dossier's primary product: a scenario-specific research brief spanning prevention, detection and suppression, with advanced scientific comparison underneath. Strengthen the operation from **“find relevant evidence and list differences”** to **“establish exactly what can transfer from a source experiment or published model to a requested use, under which physical assumptions, and what changes when those assumptions change.”**

This is an evidence-transfer assessment, not a transfer of an observed fire into an invented numerical habitat prediction. It should give positive results when support exists, reject only the unsupported stronger inference, and preserve useful mechanism-level findings.

The key correction is two-sided. Similarity of material, oxygen and flow does not establish comparability. But it is also wrong to reject all cross-environment use: published, regime-specific correlations and pressure-modelling results exist. Their explicit applicability, limitations and reproduction status should be represented rather than replaced by a blanket warning. [F03, F04, F22]

The selected contribution has three layers, not three applications:

1. **A reusable evidence and applicability registry.** Source findings, measurement definitions, experimental lineage, physical regimes and published transfer models have versioned records.
2. **A small claim-specific engine.** It selects a defensible route, checks prerequisites, performs an approved operation when the inputs and method are actually available, and exposes the exact unmet or contradicted requirement otherwise.
3. **The existing SI-powered Explore / Analysis workspace.** It summarizes, ranks and interprets the evidence, helps resolve consequential ambiguity and exports the result and revision difference.

The central user question becomes:

> Can these NASA findings or this published model help answer my research question under these conditions? Which results carry over, which do not, and what would make a stronger comparison possible?

The contribution is **not established novelty or SOTA**. Our research hypothesis is that mechanism- and regime-aware evidence transfer, combined with correct revision, provides more useful correct answers at a controlled error/cost/latency budget than equally informed alternatives. It is falsifiable. The word “graph” or the number of variables does not make it true.

## 3. The physics that changes the design

### 3.1 A flame is a coupled feedback process

For a solid, heating, decomposition, gaseous-fuel transport, mixing, reaction and heat return to unburned material interact. A reduced model is useful only when the neglected processes are small for the intended output. Thin/thick, opposed/concurrent and near-limit/away-from-limit labels are therefore model-domain information, not decorative metadata. [F19, F20, F21]

A schematic thin-layer energy bookkeeping expression is:

`storage = convection + absorbed flame radiation + imposed heating − reradiation − backing losses − effective decomposition demand`.

It is not a universal solver. Melting, charring, internal conduction, changing geometry and gas/solid coupling can require richer equations. The purpose of the schematic is to identify which assumptions a comparison consumes.

### 3.2 Flow can strengthen, weaken, or change the mode of spread

The established opposed-flow regime picture includes low-flow radiative loss, an intermediate thermal regime and a high-flow finite-kinetics limit. The appropriate model is not determined by velocity alone; material, oxygen, pressure and thermal boundaries matter. [F19]

The newer ISS filter-paper report documents low-flow flamelet spread outside a conventional planar model's predicted boundary. This changes model-form applicability, not merely the value of a coefficient. The earlier related FLARE publication and later report must not be counted as independent campaigns without checking their underlying runs. [F08, F09]

**Product consequence:** preserve observed mode and the model's dimensional/steadiness assumptions. Do not treat a failed planar approximation as proof that combustion cannot occur.

### 3.3 Confinement is an active physical variable

The confinement experiments show competing effects of baffle spacing and radiative boundaries. The apparatus also illustrates why imposed-flow settings can differ from flow reaching the fuel. [F10]

**Product consequence:** comparisons may need clearance, wall properties, holder/backing geometry and local-flow definition. These variables cannot be erased by matching an upstream velocity. The proposed registry keeps the source's measurement operator and configuration, rather than asserting a universal confinement correction.

### 3.4 Gravity is a vector and a platform, not a slider

Concurrent PMMA-rod partial-gravity results and downward thin-fuel results address different configurations and endpoints. An optimum observed in one cannot become a universal “worst gravity” for all materials. [F05, F06]

A rotating centrifuge adds spatial acceleration variation and Coriolis/recirculation effects. Its nominal gravity magnitude is not a complete substitute for uniform surface gravity. The LPSC account also distinguishes short-duration platforms from the intended long-duration research. [F11, F12]

**Product consequence:** record platform, orientation, duration and relevant acceleration/flow history. Do not place centrifuge, aircraft, orbital and future lunar measurements in one unqualified column named “gravity.”

### 3.5 Pressure is not just oxygen supply

Pressure affects density and transport alongside reaction and radiation. Constant oxygen partial pressure does not preserve all those mechanisms. [F02]

There are useful positive results: a 2025 concurrent thin-solid study correlates data across gravity environments using boundary-layer/standoff reasoning, explicitly away from extinction. A separate numerical study compares pressure–gravity scalings and finds both usefulness and residual mismatch. [F03, F04]

**Product consequence:** encode a published correlation's actual joint domain and failure region. Preserve the distinction between a reported relationship, a faithfully reproduced calculation and an independently evaluated prediction.

### 3.6 Thickness, history and material identity are coupled

New quiescent acrylic work demonstrates that thickness-dependent outcomes and time evolution matter even without imposed flow. Rod studies additionally distinguish spread cessation from later regression-flame extinction. [F07, F32–F34]

Wire-core heat conduction, melting geometry and porous smoldering introduce further mechanisms absent from a simple homogeneous sheet model. They belong in conditional branches, not an ever-growing “polymer” pooled dataset. [F16–F18]

**Product consequence:** material name is only an entry point. Use grade, geometry, thermal state, history and the output being measured. Do not declare a material intrinsically thermally thin without a timescale and model definition.

### 3.7 Detection and suppression need their own measurement chains

SAME relates controlled smoke production, aging and specific detector responses. A material's flame-spread evidence alone does not establish detection performance. [F14]

FLEX diluent studies show why gas identity and the definition of extinction matter for cool-flame droplets. Water-mist research introduces delivery, droplet and evaporation variables, but its endpoint and fuel configuration must remain attached. [F13, F15, F39]

**Product consequence:** connect prevention, detection and suppression at the level of the research question, without multiplying unrelated results into a numerical spacecraft-risk score. A linked brief is not a coupled fire simulation.

## 4. A disciplined variables model

The complete dictionary is provided separately as Markdown and JSON. Its organization deliberately distinguishes:

- **Inputs and boundary conditions:** pressure, composition, imposed flow, geometry, external heating, gravity platform.
- **Measured evolving state:** actual atmosphere, velocity field, thermal state, smoke history.
- **Outputs:** spread, regression, chemical release, detector response or an explicitly defined extinction event.
- **Derived diagnostics:** partial pressure, Reynolds/Péclet/Grashof/Richardson/Damköhler numbers and thermal-thickness ratios.
- **Evidence and protocol:** measurement operator, calibration, intervention sequence, source ancestry, model fits and versions.

A measured flame temperature or spread rate must not leak into a predictor as though it were a pre-experiment input. The same quantity can be an input to a conditional model and an output for another model; the record must say which.

| Specialist family | Examples of consequential information | Required only when |
|---|---|---|
| Atmosphere/thermodynamics | Absolute pressure, oxygen basis, diluent species, temperature, evolving composition | A physical comparison depends on them |
| Gravity/platform mechanics | Vector, orientation, jitter/transition, duration, rotation | Transfer across gravity/platforms is requested |
| Fluid mechanics | Local profile, boundary-layer length, imposed versus reacting flow, shear, recirculation | Flow-regime applicability is relevant |
| Confinement/geometry | Clearance, exposed faces, holder, wall spectrum, free volume | Geometry or enclosure affects the requested observable |
| Material/chemistry | Formulation, thermal properties, pyrolysis, char, melt, wire core, porosity | A model or comparison consumes those properties |
| Thermal feedback | External flux, conduction, radiation, backing loss, preheat length | Heat-feedback assumptions are used |
| History/protocol | Igniter, reuse, atmosphere renewal, interventions, censoring | Sequential or transient evidence is used |
| Flame outcomes | Rate/statistic, standoff, mode, chemical versus sensible heat, endpoint | Reporting or comparing results |
| Aerosol/detection | Size moment, morphology, aging, sampling loss, instrument/algorithm | Detector or smoke claims |
| Suppression/recovery | Agent, delivery, droplet distribution, endpoints, products/reignition | Suppression or cleanup research |
| Metrology/model lineage | Operators, clocks, fits, uncertainty, artifact linkage | Any quantitative interpretation |
| Derived groups | Declared scales, property evaluation and parameter conventions | A supported regime/scaling model needs them |

A small request should remain small. An explicit task-to-variable dependency list determines which missing fact matters next. We should not ask about a centrifuge rotation rate when answering a material-independent catalogue question.

## 5. What the mathematics supports—and does not

### 5.1 Partial pressure is a contextual calculation

For a declared mole fraction, `p_O2 = x_O2 P`.

The illustrative combinations 101.3 kPa × 0.21 and 60 kPa × 0.354 give 21.273 and 21.240 kPa. Their difference is about 0.155%. That establishes nearly matching partial pressures only. It does not establish equal heat transfer, kinetics, spread, detector performance or suppression response. These are not claimed matched experimental rows.

### 5.2 Pressure modelling: a useful positive route with a domain

At fixed reference composition, temperature, characteristic length and approximate viscosity/expansion behaviour:

`Re = rho U L / mu`

`Gr = g beta DeltaT L^3 rho^2 / mu^2`

With the ideal-gas density dependence in that restricted construction, `Gr` scales as `g P^2`. This explains the appeal of a pressure–gravity similarity parameter. It is a dimensional diagnostic; flame-scale property variation and chemistry can invalidate a naïve use of it.

For a hypothetical target acceleration ratio 0.165, a same-composition formal `P^2 g` match in a reference-gravity environment gives normalized pressure ratio `sqrt(0.165) = 0.4062`. A `P^1.8 g` construction gives `0.165^(1/1.8) = 0.3675`, about 9.5% lower. These are arithmetic illustrations of two scaling definitions, not test settings, an extinction prediction or advice for a crew atmosphere. The numerical study motivates comparing the assumptions; it does not make either exponent universal. [F04]

Matching a chosen invariant does not prove simultaneous matching of reaction rates, residence times, spectral losses, oxygen concentration, morphology and endpoints. Equally, differences in raw pressure do not automatically make an appropriate published scaling useless. The scientific task is to select the correct model of comparison.

### 5.3 Other diagnostics must name their scales

`Pe_heat = U L / alpha_g`; a species Péclet number instead uses the relevant diffusivity. A Lewis number is species/model-dependent. `Da = tau_flow/tau_chem` uses an explicit convention. A Richardson-like ratio `Gr/Re^2` requires a nonzero defined velocity and valid property approximations. A thermal penetration estimate `delta_s ~ sqrt(alpha_s t_heat)` makes a thermal-thickness ratio depend on exposure time as well as thickness.

These expressions organize hypotheses. They are not automatic evidence thresholds. At zero imposed velocity, infinity from a division is not an adequate description of a diffusive flame. In a rotating system, a Rossby-style comparison additionally needs angular velocity and geometric scales. [F11, F12, F19]

### 5.4 Uncertainty is part of a transformation

For a difference, `Var(B-A) = Var(B)+Var(A)-2 Cov(A,B)`. A shared sensor, calibration, source transformation or fitted parameter can connect errors. Missing covariance is not zero. Selection across alternative interpretations is not itself a statistical confidence interval. [F28]

A narrow physics module needs specified input uncertainty and an appropriate propagation method. Do not fit a convenient probability distribution merely to display a confidence band.

## 6. The specific operation to implement

### 6.1 An applicability record

A proposed record has:

- Source/finding and reviewed locator/version.
- Finding type: experiment, published model, hypothesis, or our explicit derivation.
- Quantity/endpoint/operator and unit basis.
- Joint tested conditions and actual path through them, not just independent min/max ranges.
- Physical regime and model assumptions; recorded contrary evidence or failure modes.
- Proposed use: summary, qualitative implication, descriptive comparison, model-based mapping, or new prediction.
- Required conditions and approved transformation, if one exists.
- Information-use history and uncertainty requirements.
- Access, rights, source review and reproduction status.

This extends the existing finding/model-use records rather than requiring a new graph service. The full variable dictionary is not copied into every record.

### 6.2 The output is a supported route, not a green badge

Return the original source finding regardless of whether it transfers. Then assess the requested stronger use:

1. **Directly supported description/comparison:** the reviewed evidence actually addresses the requested quantity and scope.
2. **Conditional model-based route:** a named published method may connect the settings, with required inputs, assumptions, known limitations and reproduction status. A not-yet-reproduced method is a candidate plan, not an executed result.
3. **Mechanistic/contextual use:** the study helps explain a relevant process but does not supply the requested numerical quantity.
4. **No supported route yet:** specify the known contradiction or missing requirement while retaining any useful narrower result.

These are proposed user-facing layers. Existing T1–T4 claim outcomes and AC04 remain authoritative inside the advanced assessment. No binary classification of an entire study as “good/bad” or “safe/unsafe” is introduced.

### 6.3 Three-valued conditions, plus evidence coverage

For a specified use, each necessary prerequisite is satisfied, contradicted or unknown. A contradicted prerequisite blocks that route. An unknown prerequisite leaves it unresolved. All satisfied prerequisites mean only that the implemented contract applies to the supplied records—not a proof of complete science or independent experimental validation.

Treat separately: **not in the reviewed bank**, **not retrieved**, **not publicly acquired**, and **not known to exist**. Do not infer the last from the first three. A source's independent parameter ranges do not certify all combinations in the Cartesian product. A future interval-aware calculation must account for uncertainty crossing a model-domain boundary.

### 6.4 The revision witness

When a user changes oxygen, geometry, the endpoint or the requested model claim, show:

`changed premise → affected physical assumption → affected source/method → revised permitted conclusion`.

This is a provenance-backed explanation of a decision, not a causal effect estimate. Preserve unchanged findings. Do not pretend the evidence was remeasured. Changes to a displayed mission name should not alter scientific reasoning when the underlying facts remain identical.

### 6.5 No blanket extrapolation or blanket abstention

The chief research contribution to test is balanced: accept justified conditional use while preventing unsupported transfer. A reject-everything system may have low unsupported-claim counts and no practical value. A generic RAG assistant may be fluent and transfer a conclusion too far. The engine should improve that trade-off under a fixed evidence budget; whether it does is empirical.

## 7. Three concrete task demonstrations

### Task A — Can ground pressure tests inform partial-gravity spread?

Answer positively but specifically. Identify the published thin-fuel concurrent-flow correlation route [F03] and the numerical pressure/gravity comparison [F04]. Separate their empirical or model support and domains. Ask about the desired material/configuration and whether the question concerns steady spread away from a boundary or extinction.

For an away-from-extinction matching request, return the method, required inputs and what must be reproduced. For an extinction request outside its stated domain, keep the method as context and select boundary-relevant sources instead. Do not execute an abstract-only formula with guessed constants.

**Distinct operation:** the same evidence changes from a usable conditional route to context because the requested endpoint changes—not because the study suddenly became poor quality.

### Task B — Are otherwise similar small-duct and larger-duct tests interchangeable?

Retrieve the confinement study [F10] and scale context [F20]. Preserve the original observations. Identify clearance, wall boundary and actual flow as possible comparison requirements. Do not assign the observed difference to one causal mechanism unless the design establishes it.

**Distinct operation:** the system explains why material/oxygen/nominal-velocity matching is insufficient and requests the missing apparatus facts before offering a numerical transfer. A basic question still receives the qualitative physical finding immediately.

### Task C — Does a model boundary establish no fire at very low flow?

Use the related FLARE sources [F08, F09, F36] as a counterexample to overextending a conventional planar model. Retain its valid observed domain; do not conclude all its results are wrong. Link the requested endpoint to measured flame mode and duration. A small flame below the planar prediction does not automatically validate another model.

**Distinct operation:** known model-form failure is attached to the route, and a model's global fit cannot erase the boundary exception.

The Saffire conditional-transport example remains a fourth, already researched advanced path. Detection and suppression follow-ups use their own observables [F13–F15], not an inferred fire-safety score from these three spread tasks.

These are **assistant-authored development demonstrations**. They establish source-backed design requirements, not independent evaluation or implemented performance.

## 8. SI design and the failed interpreter

Preserve the current distinction between requested intent, missing information, application capabilities and the scientific assessment. The new physics representation does not repair those language errors by itself.

The proposed bounded runtime is:

`interpret a small research goal → retrieve relevant reviewed records → clarify only a consequential ambiguity → deterministic applicability/comparison → assemble a cited answer`.

Most relations and units are checked in code. The LLM may help select candidates, interpret wording or organize approved findings. It may not approve a new physical law, create calibration history or certify independence. A second synthesis call is an explicit, costed option, not an agent loop. Human confirmation remains, and human repairs are counted separately from model success.

Do not select a provider by brand or model size. Both tested Groq configurations remain rejected under the frozen internal gate. The original failure audit still needs exact packets before asserting that a schema repair is the cause of improvement. No new inference was authorized or run in this pass. [U02]

For the research comparison, test the simple repaired pipeline and the bounded adaptive variant under matched information and budgets. A typed-form variant isolates interaction value; it is a useful fallback but not proof of live SI. Distinct scientific records should not require a new model training run.

## 9. SOTA and Pareto: define a claim that can be earned

### 9.1 Three different achievements

- **Predictive-physics SOTA:** a newly evaluated predictor beats strong physical/statistical comparators on properly separated campaigns and outputs. Not established here. A future narrow target could build on acquired multi-gravity thin-fuel data and faithfully reproduced published correlations; it needs data and validation, not merely an LLM.
- **Evidence-transfer method SOTA:** the proposed system more accurately identifies valid, conditional and unsupported use on an externally adjudicated task set against strong same-information methods. This is a plausible research target, not a current achievement or necessarily a novel mathematical algorithm.
- **System Pareto result:** no tested eligible configuration improves one declared performance objective without worsening another. This is finite, task- and competitor-relative—not “globally optimal.”

For the event, seek a functioning, measured workflow and reusable scientific assets. For a publication-quality claim, require genuinely independent task adjudication, held-out investigations/model lineages, uncertainty and reproducibility. A custom-trained model is not required to outperform on a system-level task, but performance still has to be demonstrated.

### 9.2 Quality first, then frontier

Define feasible configurations as those meeting prespecified scientific correctness and useful-completion requirements. Within that set compare a small number of objectives, such as useful correct coverage, total task latency, total API cost and researcher correction effort. Retain critical-error counts separately, including any error that reverses the physical scope or fabricates evidence.

For a common task set, configuration A dominates B only if it is no worse on every declared objective and strictly better on at least one. In an empirical study, uncertainty can make dominance unresolved. Do not declare a frontier point from a tiny difference in sample means or omit failed tasks/repair cost.

The companion arithmetic makes a useful caution concrete. On the reported old study, 20B has more fully correct distinct cases, lower estimated cost and shorter network-response median, while 120B has fewer critical-error calls. Under those four chosen raw objectives both are nondominated. **Neither meets the quality gate, so the acceptable set is empty.** Calling that raw pair “our winning Pareto frontier” would be misleading. Different prompts/effort settings and correlated cases further limit interpretation. [U02]

### 9.3 Strong comparisons and ablations

Use identical reviewed sources, applicability assertions and historical-use information for:

1. A well-prepared worksheet plus a capable scientific assistant.
2. Lexical/structured retrieval with ordinary source-grounded answering.
3. A strong retrieval/synthesis pipeline in the OpenScholar/PaperQA class when its implementation and allowed budget can actually be reproduced.
4. IGNIS's regime/endpoint/domain-aware selection and revision.

Separately compare end-to-end ingestion from the same original documents and charge all curation. Do not give only IGNIS the manually recovered counterexamples.

Ablate physical regime conditions, endpoint definitions, counterevidence retrieval, information-use checks, automatic revision and runtime AI one at a time where meaningful. A single aggregate “quality” number can conceal harm: report useful correct completion, unsupported transfer, needless exclusion, source/quantity binding, correction effort and costs together.

### 9.4 Evaluation design

Legacy question variants are regression cases. Freeze new tasks before model tuning and group them by investigation, underlying burn/model and calibration lineage. The 2023/2024/2026 FLARE accounts may overlap; do not put one in training and another in a supposed independent holdout without checking run identity.

Use expert labels for claim scope and permitted numerical use where feasible. Without them, call the result an internal development study. A few successful tasks can demonstrate an operation but do not establish universal accuracy. Mathematically, even 0 observed failures in 24 independent identical Bernoulli trials would have a one-sided 95% upper error bound around 11.7%; the correlated current tasks do not justify applying that interval directly.

A new external test should include positive transfer, known invalid transfer, genuine ambiguity, ordinary lookup, unseen combinations of supported conditions, and corpus-scoped missingness. For numerical models, separate within-domain interpolation, near-boundary behaviour and out-of-domain response. No claimed winner may owe its score to blanket refusal.

No new spending, credential use or API calls are included in this dossier. The next comparison needs an explicit scope and approved consumption/call cap.

## 10. Prior art, adoption and the reusable artifact

MicroFire already describes mission-oriented evidence, gaps and research planning. A newly found FIRE-X README also claims broad data/RAG/visualization capabilities. These descriptions were not reproduced or accepted as scientific proof; they are enough to reject the assumption that broad coverage or a graph is unique. [F30, F31]

OntoChemExp, NIST's material infrastructure, established solvers and PROV already cover substantial parts of the conceptual machinery. IGNIS should complement them, not relabel them as its invention. [F21, F25–F27]

The adoptable contribution should survive without our UI:

- A small, open, versioned registry of reviewed findings and applicability contracts.
- Exact original-source locators, units/measurement meanings and permitted-use restrictions.
- A documented format for proposed/reproduced model modules, including domain tests and invalid-use counterexamples.
- A reusable scientific test set with transparent lineage and review status.
- A simple, documented interface for checking a proposed use and obtaining the result with its justification.

No production interface exists yet; the preceding items are release targets. Code licensing does not automatically license underlying papers/images/data. Preserve item-specific rights and do not redistribute full source PDFs by default. PROV-inspired fields are not a certified conformance claim.

An initial adoption test is practical: can a researcher export one comparison, reproduce it in their own notebook/workbook, change one relevant premise and understand the changed result without us explaining it? Demonstrated recurring benefit matters more than a promised NASA partnership. Existing NASA synthesis work establishes a relevant workload, not demand for this particular implementation. [F22]

Do not impose a permanent ban on numerical recovery as a scientific principle. Legitimate author data, lawful accessible supplementary material or carefully validated digitization can expand coverage. Any change to the project's current “no digitization” policy must be explicit and versioned. Digitized published curves remain processed estimates with extraction uncertainty, not raw sensor histories. No such extraction was performed here.

## 11. A product that serves all five judging criteria

The supplied 2026 judging guide scores Impact, Creativity, Validity, Relevance and Presentation. It does not justify assigning ourselves five-out-of-five scores. [U04]

| Criterion | Build evidence rather than a slogan | Main risk |
|---|---|---|
| Impact | A researcher completes a consequential evidence-transfer or test-selection task and reuses the export | An elegant tool for an infrequent task nobody adopts |
| Creativity | A meaningful physical-domain/revision capability demonstrated against strong alternatives | Calling established provenance or RAG novel |
| Validity | Source-backed regimes, correct quantities, useful conditional results and executed tests | Polished extrapolation, fabricated curves or unearned independent-validation language |
| Relevance | NASA findings remain central; summary/ranking/interpretation works across the requested safety areas | Returning to a model-only form or drifting into generic literature QA |
| Presentation | One clear question, useful result, changed condition, exact evidence and reusable export | A wall of graphs, badges and caveats with no scientific payoff |

A presentation story can begin with “When can Earth fire evidence inform space?” It should then show a supported route and a meaningful limit, not claim that every Earth test is misleading. Do not show unearned safe/unsafe maps. The supplied limit remains seven slides including title/intro, or a video no longer than thirty seconds; the complete public submission carries the substantive evidence. [U05]

The UI can show observed points/conditions, categorical regime annotations, source-domain notes and comparison tables. A rectangular min/max envelope must be labelled marginal ranges unless actual joint coverage is established. An interactive control changes evidence selection or a supported model input; it must not silently invent a response surface.

| The Pareto frontier rewards an inaccurate low-cost system | Critical | Apply quality constraints before frontier selection and disclose uncertainty/failed tasks. |

**Review result:** scientifically credible design direction and a sharper research contribution to test. **Not passed:** software performance, model reproduction, independent scientific review, adoption, SOTA or eligibility. These are different gates; additional prose cannot close them.

## 13. Event demonstration versus research programme

The event minimum is one complete source-backed research brief, one meaningful physics-sensitive revision, one advanced comparison, and sourced detection/suppression follow-ups. It must work with live SI on a fresh question, and produce a consistent accessible export. It should expose the actual corpus size and review level, not compete on unverified row counts.

Within the inherited 23 productive hours, replace rather than add scope: source/contract subset (4), engine and scientific regression cases (4), UI/export integration (5), bounded AI route (3), end-to-end checking (4), deployment/submission/disclosure (3). These are effort assumptions only. Reconcile with the actual remaining authorized window; do not count optional new research as free event time.

A stronger research milestone is a **single faithfully reproduced published transfer model** with its native-domain benchmark, known failure examples and versioned interface. That milestone is not complete here: accessible abstracts and indexed equations do not establish all coefficients, units, processing and data. Acquire the exact method/data, verify it, then execute it. No requirement to train a foundation model is involved.

A later predictive programme could compare published physical correlations, a simple transparent fit and a richer model on acquired independent campaign data. That is a plausible narrow SOTA route, not a promised universal predictor. Its endpoint must be chosen after data access, not before.

## 14. Final decision and the highest-value next action

Keep the current product hierarchy. Add **physics- and domain-aware evidence transfer** as the central analytical distinction. Keep the Saffire contracts, but stop treating all non-identical conditions as either fully comparable or wholly unusable.

The highest-value next action is a source-to-result vertical slice: one positive away-from-boundary transfer candidate, its near-limit counterexample, and a question revision that changes the applicable route while preserving the sources. If the exact numerical method remains inaccessible, demonstrate the honest candidate-plan result and choose an already supported calculation for the numerical path. Do not fake execution.

For research ambition, invest in the model/data acquisition needed to reproduce one published transfer law. For competition delivery, prioritize a complete, intelligible NASA workflow and honest measured behaviour. For adoption, publish the reusable evidence contracts and tests so a scientist can build on the work without adopting our entire interface.

**Success is not “a dashboard with more fields.” It is a demonstrably better way to determine what space-fire evidence supports, where it transfers, and where a change of physical regime breaks that transfer.**
